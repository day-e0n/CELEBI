Sirius GPU page cache · ClickBench · 6 GiB budget

Where the 6 GiB cache budget goes

Uniform 16 MiB pages give 0.53 GiB to padding and lose nothing to external fragmentation, so they hold the most cached data: 5.42 GiB of 6. Exact-size pages lose 0.75 GiB to fragmentation. Pages also serve 1.6 times as many bytes from the cache as whole batches do: 90.7 GiB against 55.6 GiB.

Most of what enters the cache is never read again

The 495 scans ask for 503.5 GiB; the cache holds 6. In every design, 92 to 94% of the bytes evicted were never read from the cache, and at any moment 3.5 to 4.1 GiB of the budget holds such data. Uniform pages remove the fragmentation and its extra evictions, but serve the same bytes as exact-size pages: 90.7 GiB.

How this was measured

  • The scan sequence of a real ClickBench run (41 queries × 3 runs, cache off) is replayed against a cudaMallocAsync pool, the allocator Sirius uses, capped at 6 GiB.
  • Sizes are the decoded bytes of each column in each row group, read from the parquet file: data, null mask and string offsets.
  • Batch: one cache entry per scan batch and column, stored the way cuDF holds a column (data, mask and offsets as separate allocations). Exact-size pages: up to 16 MiB of data per page, mask and offsets allocated separately. Uniform 16 MiB pages: one 16 MiB allocation per page holding data, mask and offsets, the rest padding.
  • When the cache is full, the least recently used entry is evicted. If the bytes fit the budget but the pool cannot place an allocation, one more entry is evicted; that is the extra eviction from fragmentation.
  • External fragmentation is the free memory in the pool minus the largest block it can still allocate in one piece, found by trying allocations at 2 MiB resolution after every scan.

Limits

  • The replay gives the cache its own 6 GiB pool. In Sirius the cache shares one pool with query execution.
  • Sizes are whole row groups and ignore filters, and any repeat of a cache key is served from the cache. Served bytes are an upper bound on reuse, not Sirius's own figures.
  • String page boundaries come from each row group's average row length.
